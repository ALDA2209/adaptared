How it works

AdaptaRed turns the weather forecast into a decision: which community is at greatest risk, why, and what to do. Every calculation is in plain view so a technician can review and adjust it.

The risk score

We follow the IPCC framework: risk depends on the hazard, on how many people and livelihoods are exposed, and on how vulnerable they are.

Risk = 100 x hazard x (0.4 + 0.6 x exposure) x (0.4 + 0.6 x vulnerability)

Hazard, exposure and vulnerability range from 0 to 1. A community's overall risk is the one from its highest hazard.

How each hazard is calculated

{{ icon("calor", "h-6 w-6") }}
Extreme heat
Highest average over three consecutive days in which the maximum temperature exceeds the normal for the area. It starts to count from a 1.5 °C difference and reaches its cap at about 6.5 °C.
{{ icon("inundacion", "h-6 w-6") }}
Flooding
Maximum rainfall over three days in the forecast, adjusted for how saturated the soil already is. About 90 mm in three days on wet soil is the cap.
{{ icon("sequia", "h-6 w-6") }}
Drought
Rain deficit over the last 30 days compared with normal (60%) and current soil moisture (40%).

Where the real data would come from

  • Weather: today the "Compare with live data" button queries Open-Meteo. Ideally we would add SENAMHI stations and satellite rainfall (CHIRPS).
  • Population and vulnerability: INEI censuses and each municipality's health and water records.
  • Crops: Sentinel-2 vegetation indices and planting calendars from the agricultural agency.
  • Local events: community reports, verified by a technician.

Limits you should know about

  • The data in this demonstration is simulated. Thresholds must be calibrated with historical events from each zone.
  • The 7-day forecast has uncertainty, especially for rainfall in the mountains.
  • The agriculture simulator is illustrative and needs field data to give harvest figures.
  • It does not replace Civil Defense or SENAMHI. It complements their information with local decisions.

Data and community

  • Full names, ID numbers and phone numbers of the people who report are not stored.
  • The data of each community belongs to the community and its municipality.
  • Alerts are reviewed with local people before they are sent at large scale.
